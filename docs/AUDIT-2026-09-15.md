## Method

1. Ran the full verification suite fresh: `tsc --noEmit`, `oxlint src/`, `npm test` (Vitest), `go build ./...`, `go vet ./...`, `go test -race -short ./...`, and the full `go test ./...` (real Postgres via testcontainers, no mocks).
2. Cross-checked every `[x]` and `[ ]` line in every `docs/tasks/Mx-*.md` against the actual source — reading the referenced files directly, not just grepping for filenames or class names existing.
3. Used parallel research passes for M8–M11, M12/M15/M16, and M17/M18 to get independent verification; resolved the one disagreement between passes (a `solidarity_pool_test.go` claim) by reading the file directly myself.
4. Archived milestones only after confirming zero gaps — not just "checkboxes now say `[x]`."

## The Two Findings Worth Reading Even If You Skip Everything Else

**1. The Global Solidarity Pool (M11) doesn't do anything in real gameplay.** `GET /api/v1/district/resilience` computes its answer from a `crisis_log` database table that nothing in production ever writes to — only the test fixture does. The endpoint, its Go tests, and the HUD badge that renders it are all individually correct and well-tested; the whole feature is just disconnected from the thing that's supposed to feed it. This is the clearest example in the repo of "the tests pass" not meaning "the feature works" — worth internalizing as a pattern to watch for elsewhere, not just fixing this one instance.

**2. The Living Economy (M8) and the AI Narrative pipeline's Broadsheet headline (M9) are both effectively cosmetic today.** M8's economy runs 100% on a synthetic sinusoidal calendar — no live data source was ever wired in, despite that being the milestone's entire premise. M9's real, working, AI-backed `/api/v1/narrative/daily-scenarios` endpoint exists and is genuinely used — just not by the one player-facing surface (the Broadsheet headline) that's supposed to showcase it; that surface is 4 hardcoded string templates instead. Neither is broken, both are well-built in isolation — they're just not connected to the parts of the game that were supposed to make them visible.
